Pangea — non-custodial architecture

How a donation happens without Pangea holding your key

Short version: Pangea never needs your private key because Pangea never signs anything. Your phone signs. The blockchain checks the signature. Here's the mechanism, step by step.

The short answer

Your phone generates a signing key the moment you first log in, and that key is encrypted inside Android's SecureStore — it is never sent anywhere, not even to Pangea's own servers. When you donate, your phone uses that key to sign the transaction on the device. What travels off the phone is the finished, signed transaction — not the key. Polygon's network then verifies that signature itself. Pangea's backend isn't part of that check at all; it just watches the public blockchain afterward and updates its own records to match.

Setup — the one time your phone and Pangea talk about identity

This happens once, the first time you log in on a device. It only establishes who you are. No money, no signing key, ever crosses to Pangea's side.

Every donation — signing happens on the phone, verification happens on-chain

This is the part that matters: notice there is no arrow from Pangea's servers into the transaction itself. Pangea is upstream of it (login) and downstream of it (reading the result) — never inside it.

This is what "non-custodial" means in practice for Pangea, not just as a slogan: there is no step in this diagram where a server could sign, redirect, or block a donation, because no server ever holds the thing needed to sign one. Polygon's EntryPoint and your smart-account contract are the only judges of whether a transaction is valid — and they only accept it if it was signed by the key sitting in SecureStore on your phone.
